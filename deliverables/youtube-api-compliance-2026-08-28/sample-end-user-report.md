## Structured rehabilitation options — example research summary

### Bottom line

- The inspected sources describe two materially different rehabilitation
  programs. The example evidence is sufficient to describe them separately,
  but not to recommend one for an individual.
- Public YouTube discussion is used to look for implementation details,
  reported failures, harms, and durability questions. It is not treated as a
  controlled estimate of benefit.

### Approaches compared

| Approach | What was actually examined | Example outcome and horizon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Progressive resistance program | Supervised lower-body resistance sessions, three times weekly for 12 weeks, with progression rules | Walking tolerance and function at 12 weeks |
| Aquatic mobility program | Pool-based mobility and conditioning sessions, twice weekly for 10 weeks | Pain during activity and function at 10 weeks |

### What the evidence found

The synthetic formal-evidence example reports different short-term outcomes for
the two exact programs. This result cannot be generalized to programs with a
different dose, disease stage, supervision level, or comparator. No individual
treatment recommendation is authorized by this example.

### What remains uncertain

- Whether either exact program applies to a different disease stage or baseline.
- Durability beyond the synthetic follow-up period.
- The frequency of benefits, failures, or harms in the wider population.
- Public comments are not representative, and unavailable or moderated material
  cannot be observed through the API.

### Questions for clinician review

- Does the evidence population match the person’s actual stage and constraints?
- Are the program dose and supervision feasible and safe?
- Which outcomes and harms should be monitored, and over what period?
