    Why the time matters, in numbers

    In the trials of the clot-dissolving drug (a drug that breaks up the blood clot blocking the brain), people treated within 3 hours did best: 33 in 100 recovered with little or no disability, against 23 in 100 without it. After 4.5 hours those trials showed no clear gain. The drug also caused a deadly bleed in the brain in about 3 in 100 people, against fewer than 1 in 100 without it, which is why hospitals scan the brain first. Other stroke treatments have other time limits. Sooner is better for all of them.

    Call now, even though it passed.

    Call now, even though it passed

    Sudden weakness, a drooping face, trouble speaking, or sudden loss of sight or balance can warn of a stroke, even if it stops within minutes. Doctors call this a warning stroke.

    The first days after carry the most risk; in one UK study, fast care cut strokes in the next 3 months from 10 in 100 to 2 in 100.

    Many callers turn out not to have had a stroke, often about half, and that is expected.

    A false alarm usually means a scan and some tests; a missed warning can mean a stroke.

    • If the signs stopped only minutes or hours ago, or it was a sudden worst-ever headache, call the emergency number now (911 in the US, 112 in Europe, 999 in the UK). Why: health services ask for an emergency call for stroke signs even when they go away, and a sudden worst-ever headache can be bleeding in the brain even after it eases.
    • Otherwise, phone an emergency department or a stroke clinic now and say: possible warning stroke. Why: those words tell them what to check, and UK guidance asks for a stroke specialist within 24 hours.
    • Go where they send you. Why: they know which place can do the tests soonest.
    • Don't drive yourself. Why: the signs could come back at the wheel.
    • If any sign comes back, call the emergency number (911 in the US, 112 in Europe, 999 in the UK). Why: signs happening now are an emergency (line 4, just above), and some treatments work only within hours.
    Why call when it has passed, in numbers

    A warning stroke means stroke signs that pass. After one, about 5 in 100 people have a stroke within a week. It was 11 in 100 where nobody treated it urgently, and 1 in 100 where a specialist stroke service treated it as an emergency (18 studies, 10,126 people).

    The UK study was in Oxford. Seeing people within a day instead of about three, and starting treatment at once, went with a fall in strokes over the next 3 months from 10 in 100 to 2 in 100. It compared before with after; it was not a fair test where chance decides who gets what.

    Why a false alarm is expected: the face, arm and speech signs catch strokes, but they do not rule out other causes. Of 100 people who call after signs pass, 40 to 67 turn out to have had a warning stroke or a small stroke (three studies). The others usually get a brain scan and tests, and often learn the real cause, such as an infection, a faint or a migraine. The clot-dissolving drug (it breaks up a clot in the brain) is given only while signs last, so people whose signs have passed almost never get it.

    Most strokes give no signs anyone notices: one US estimate counted about 11 million silent strokes in one year (1998), against 770,000 with signs. This line is about the ones that do give signs.

    The bigger risk is losing the hours when a treatment still works, or missing bleeding in the brain, because the signs eased enough to feel reassuring.

    If it was more than a week ago, it is less pressing, but UK stroke guidance still asks for a specialist within seven days.

How to change things

  • Change everything that is obviously harmful now, all at once, as soon as it is safe: tap water, junk food, caffeine, drugs such as cocaine, sleeping in a moldy room. Why: there is no point waiting weeks between these.
  • Where stopping fast is not safe, go slowly, with a plan from whoever prescribes it or a clinic: heavy daily drinking, sleeping or anxiety pills (benzodiazepines), opioids, and any prescribed medicine. Why: stopping some of these suddenly can cause seizures or severe withdrawal.
  • Try things that might or might not help, such as supplements, one at a time. Why: if you start several at once, you can't tell which one helped, or which one made you worse.
  • Start everything at the lowest dose. Stop if you react badly. Why: it's allergy & sensitivity testing, common sense for everything. So don't start breathing anything in as a mist until you are fine with smelling it.
  • Nothing on the get-help lists waits. Why: those signs need help now or today.

Step 1. Drink pure water

What do you drink most days?

Drink filtered water. Tap water is almost never drinkable these days.

Quick fixToday, drink water from a good reverse-osmosis (R/O) filter, distilled water, or tested spring, well or rain water.
Root causeFit a good R/O filter for drinking, and a whole-house filter, so you aren't poisoning yourself in the shower and when washing dishes.
Why

Water must be filtered correctly. Tap water can carry fluoride, chlorine, microplastics, PFAS, lead, arsenic and nitrate. Drink water itself, not only other drinks: many people hardly drink water at all. Distilled water (boiled to steam and cooled again) has no minerals, so add minerals sometimes. Bottled water can take on its plastic: try smelling the water in an older plastic bottle.

How well studied

Personal field signal: what the author and others have noticed, not tested in studies.

Background

In 2015 nearly 21 million Americans were on water systems that broke health standards. At least one PFAS chemical (known as forever chemicals: man-made chemicals that barely break down) can be found in about 45% of US tap samples. These show that contamination is common. They don't show how often tap water is unsafe to drink, and they say nothing about your own tap.

Where most doctors and officials differ

Regulators call tap water that meets their standards safe to drink.

Risk

Everyday risk: widely done, with no serious harm on record.

Step 2. Get deep sleep, and stop over-breathing

Deep sleep is the body's main detox and healing time, and it gets harder to get every year. First, fix underlying medical problems, starting with sleep apnea. Then keep a two-week log and build steady habits. And stop over-breathing and mouth-breathing: many people breathe through the mouth at night, and over-breathe in the day partly because of it.

Sleepy driving is as dangerous as drunk driving, and caffeine is a fake fix. Get help sooner: with drowsy driving, consider seeing a specialist rather than tinkering with different reddit hacks.

First, check for sleep apnea: breathing that stops and starts in your sleep.

Quick fixTonight, record your breathing through the night with a simple microphone: your own sleep study. If you hear snoring or pauses in breathing, that's a huge clue. Also film yourself to see if your mouth is open, and ask your partner if you snore, or use a phone app.
Root causeIf you do have apnea, confirm which kind (a blocked airway, the brain's control of breathing, or a mix) and how severe it is: a sleep test shows this. Then consider first: lose weight if overweight, less alcohol and sedatives (calming or sleeping pills), more exercise (Yin yoga especially: slow yoga with long, held stretches), myofunctional therapy (exercises for the tongue, mouth and face), and fixing what blocks the airway.
Why

You might feel like you breathe fine right now, but the real test is when you're asleep. The most common medical treatment is a CPAP machine, which forces air in through a mask (it needs a prescription). Many people can't tolerate it, and it's not a true cure, so consider the root-cause steps first.

How well studied

Recording yourself and listening: Personal field signal, not tested in studies. The machine (CPAP): Limited human evidence. In before-and-after studies the car-crash rate was about 70% lower on it, and sleepiness eased after one night. That is not a promise for any one person. The root-cause list: Personal field signal.

Background

Drivers with sleep apnea crash 1.2 to 4.9 times as often (several studies of drivers, combined).

Risk

Everyday risk: widely done, with no serious harm on record.

Put a bag of it in your pillow! That is cleaned zeolite, a mineral powder, in a bag against the skin; the author found it cured his insomnia quickly. There is research on zeolite taken by mouth for sleep, but nobody has studied this through-a-bag method. Risk: no harm found, not studied. Dust from a leaking bag has not been measured.

How well studied

Personal field signal: what the author and others have noticed, not tested in studies. Not checked yet: a fixed wake time is one part of the standard talking-and-habits treatment for poor sleep (called CBT-I); by itself it was not checked here.

Background

In a 2023 review of 24 studies, caffeine cut sleep by about 45 minutes a night; a cup of coffee shortened sleep unless it was drunk at least 8.8 hours before bed. In one small study of 9 healthy men, 200 mg of caffeine taken at 7 in the morning still reduced sleep the next night.

Where most doctors and officials differ

They don't ask everyone to give up caffeine. For poor sleep, the NHS lists caffeine as one cause, and says no tea or coffee in the 6 hours before bed. The US FDA says up to 400 mg of caffeine a day is not generally linked to harm for most adults, but some conditions and medicines make people more sensitive. Europe's food safety body says up to 200 mg a day in pregnancy. None of them says anything about caffeine and sleep apnea.

Risk

Everyday risk: widely done, with no serious harm on record. Stopping caffeine is a small risk: side effects are common but pass. In experiments, about half of regular users got a headache on stopping, and 13 in 100 had distress or trouble functioning that the researchers counted as significant. It starts within a day and lasts 2 to 9 days.

It only takes a minute to check your BOLT score, to see if you are over-breathing. BOLT comes from the Buteyko method (Step 1 below).

  • Sit still. After a normal breath out through your nose, pinch your nose shut. Count the seconds until you first want to breathe. Why: that first urge is the score, not how long you can hold out.
  • Let go at the first urge, and breathe calmly. Skip this if holding your breath makes you anxious. Why: it is a quick measure, not a breath-holding practice.

A healthy adult has a score of 40+. Below 20 is a sure indicator of chronic anxiety, sleep and health problems. These are the guide's numbers. The test itself has not been shown to measure over-breathing: researchers who tried it in 49 athletes say it has not been scientifically validated.

Stressed right now? Purse your lips and slow down the breath out. Don't breathe in more to make up for it.

The slight air hunger makes you a little anxious for the first 20 seconds or so; after that, you'll feel much calmer.

  • Don't hold your breath. Why: this only slows the breath out; it never stops it.
  • Dizzy or tingly? Stop the exercise, sit or lie down, and breathe normally. Why: breathing too much can cause this, but so can other things. If it doesn't pass, or it gets worse, get help; if a sign from the safety check appears, follow it now.
Why

Slower breaths out, without breathing more on the way in, mean less breathing overall. The slight air hunger (a mild wish for more air) makes you a little anxious for the first 20 seconds or so, but after that, you'll feel much calmer. This targets one actual cause of anxiety: over-breathing. You can use this any time, not just in a therapy session. It is Buteyko reduced breathing (Step 1 below).

How well studied

Personal field signal: what the author and others have noticed. No trial of it for calming was found.

Risk

No harm found, not studied for this use. Prone to panic? Go gentler at first: the first seconds of air hunger can feel like panic.

Longer-term breathing practice to try: change how you breathe all day. Step 1: Buteyko. Step 2: Systema.

Why

Over-breathing (breathing more air than the body needs) is one of the root causes of sleep apnea, a dripping nose at the back of the throat, poor sleep, anxiety that starts in the body (and can loop into the mind), asthma, and poor health in general.

How well studied

Whether this helps stress or sleep is not established by the evidence cited here. That over-breathing causes these problems is the author's explanation, not a tested one.

Step 1: Buteyko. Breathe a little less, through the nose, as a habit.

Buteyko is a method of breathing a little less than you feel you need, through the nose. It comes first because it is much easier for people to understand.

How well studied

Limited human evidence, for asthma only: breathing exercises improved quality of life (a review of 22 trials, 2,880 people; two of the trials used Buteyko), and a 60-person Buteyko trial cut inhaler use by about a fifth. For calming and sleep: Personal field signal (noticed by the author and others); no trial was found.

Risk

No harm found, not studied for this use. The review did not assess side effects, so it cannot tell us how often harms occur. Prone to panic? Go gentler at first: breathing less brings a mild wish for more air, which can feel like panic.

Step 2: Systema. Breathe lightly and never pause. Keep it going under everything you do.

Systema is a martial art built on relaxed breathing. Its breathing has no pause between breathing in and breathing out.

  • Learn it sitting or lying down first, not while driving or in water. Why: beginners sometimes breathe too big and get dizzy.
  • Dizzy or tingly? Stop the exercise, sit or lie down, and breathe normally. Why: breathing too much can cause this, but so can other things. If it doesn't pass, or it gets worse, get help; if a sign from the safety check appears, follow it now.
  • Once it is easy, keep it going all day, under everything you do. Why: it's a background process, not a task you focus on.
How

Light natural breathing, not something you try to control. Each breath is 45-80% of a full deep breath, so you don't fully exhale, you don't fully inhale either. The only control is that you make sure you are not pausing. Feel any tension in the body as you breathe, and imagine the breath dissolving it.

Why

Even better than just breathing less is circular breathing, Systema style: it can put you into the flow state very quickly. For anxiety, Systema continuous breathing is great! Most people who hear about it try it just barely and forget it, so the buttons below keep a dated log of what you tried.

A video of Systema breathing

The one the somatic guide links to, though it may not be the best one: youtube.com/watch?v=Nar2-nvomAk. It opens on YouTube, online.

How well studied

Personal field signal: what the author and the Systema community have noticed. No studies found.

Risk

No harm found, not studied. Breathing that turns big and fast can bring dizziness or tingling.

Later, with someone you trust: painless taps, to practise staying calm through fear.

  • Keep breathing lightly through every tap. Why: staying relaxed and breathing through the fear is the practice.
  • The person receiving closes their eyes. Why: so they really can't cheat: they can't see whether the next tap is coming.
What

Light taps to the belly, or light slaps to the face that don't hurt. They are PAINLESS. You don't know whether the next one will land, and even if the strikes don't land, it can still work usually.

Why

The idea is to do something that instills fear, with safety ensured: you learn to stay relaxed through fear. It is like desensitization therapies (getting used to a fear step by step), except there is no need to go back to a painful memory first.

How well studied

Personal field signal: what the author has noticed, not tested in studies. He is not a Systema master yet, and there is no written guide for this yet.

Risk

No harm found, not studied. Painless is the intent; a tap can land harder than meant, and fear can bring back old, painful fears. No rate exists for either.

Step 3. Get rid of mold

Any damp, mold or musty smell at home?

First, stop breathing in the mold. Bedrooms and laundry rooms first.

Quick fixTonight, run a third-party certified True HEPA air filter (a fine-dust filter) in the bedroom. The cheap fix is to open all the windows and doors, if you're not worried about criminals or outside pollution.
Root causeDehumidify, but keep enough humidity for your nose and sinuses, with a humidity meter (hygrometer) by your bed. If the mold is in the walls, think first: remediating a moldy house can be super expensive and can backfire if not done intelligently, and moving to a house without mold is sometimes cheaper.
Why

Prevent breathing in the mold that's there, above all in the bedroom: you need fresh clean air to heal while in deep sleep. Ideally the filter is connected to a ventilator that brings in filtered fresh air from outside, since even filtered indoor air can still be stale. Mold grows best above 50-60% humidity, and below 40% most people get problems from irritated noses and sinuses. Try to aim for the lowest humidity that doesn't cause your sinuses to feel too dry; a small humidifier near your bed can help while the rest of the house stays drier. Wash all clothes and linens in closets regularly, even if unused, especially if they smell at all off. Sunlight won't do it: it doesn't remove the mold's toxins (mycotoxins). Air conditioners are a huge mold problem: even self-cleaning ones need checking every so often for mold in the water trays, tubes and cooling fins.

How well studied

Personal field signal: what the author and others have noticed, not tested in studies.

Background

Homes with damp or mold go with 30-50% more breathing and asthma problems. That shows only that the two go together; it does not test these steps.

Risk

Everyday risk: widely done, with no serious harm on record.

Step 4. Get your sense of smell back

Is your sense of smell weaker than it was?

Clear away artificial scents. Then find what weakened your smell.

Quick fixToday, no air fresheners or scented laundry products. Stop using dish sponges, switch to nylon brushes.
Root causeFind what weakened your sense of smell: mold (step 3), sinus trouble, or cooking oils gone bad (rancid seed oils). For sinus trouble, nasal irrigation with Quinton Seawater is one of the best solutions: it is not just salt water.
  • If you rinse your nose, use ready-made sterile saline sold for the nose, or a nasal-rinse sachet mixed as its packet says with distilled or sterile water, or with tap water boiled for 3 to 5 minutes and cooled. Never use water straight from the tap. After each rinse, clean the device with the same kind of water and let it air-dry. Why: tap water in the nose has, rarely, carried a brain infection.
Why

Almost tied with improving sleep is improving your sense of smell and taste. If your sense of smell is bad, you can't tell when things are moldy or plastic-y; some people still can, but are so used to it that they no longer notice. Artificial scents, air fresheners, are designed to kill you!

How well studied

Personal field signal: what the author and others have noticed, not tested in studies. The claim about artificial scents: Not checked yet. The nose rinse: Limited human evidence (two small trials of salt-water rinsing in 116 adults; seawater itself was not tested).

Background

Among 3,005 Americans aged 57 to 85, those who could not smell died at four times the rate over the next five years. That shows only that the two go together; it does not show that clearing scents helps.

Where most doctors and officials differ

A loss of smell that lasts deserves a doctor's look.

Risk

Everyday risk: widely done, with no serious harm on record. The nose rinse is a small risk: side effects are common but pass, and a rare serious one can be avoided. In one six-month trial of a strong salt-water rinse, 23 in 100 had side effects, nosebleeds among them. The brain infection by tiny water organisms (amoebas) after nose rinsing: 10 US cases from 1994 to 2022, all in people with weak immune systems, many using tap water. No rate per rinse exists.

Step 5. Exercise

Move every day: aerobic exercise and strength training.

Quick fixToday, move more than you usually do: a walk, a swim or a workout, at a pace you can manage.
Root causeMake aerobic exercise and strength training part of every week. Cut down on carbs, sugary drinks (liquid fructose), and especially alcohol, which build fat around the organs.
  • Chest pain, pressure, faintness or unusual breathlessness when you exert yourself? Stop, and follow line 3 of the safety check now. Why: it can be the heart.
Why

Exercise does things that no diet or supplement can copy. Movement is the main pump of the lymph system, which carries waste away from the tissues. Aerobic exercise and strength training help the liver, and reduce the fat around the liver and other organs, fat that also stores toxins. Sweating is a toxin clearance system par excellence: sweat from exercise clears toxic metals like lead and cadmium better than sitting in a hot car. Working out also speeds up the brain's own waste clearance.
