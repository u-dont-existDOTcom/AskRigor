Healthy living: pilot 1

This is a pilot: a first test version, being tried before anyone relies on it. It has not yet been tested with the people it is meant for.

It is not personal medical advice. It does not know you, cannot examine you, and cannot tell you what is wrong. It is not a diagnosis, and it does not replace a doctor.

What it is: short things to consider, taken from the health guides of the guides' author, for one adult answering for themselves. Each one shows how well it has been studied.

Breathing

Chest

Brain

    • Note the time it started. Why: some stroke treatments can only be given within hours of the start.
    Why the time matters, in numbers

    In the trials of the clot-dissolving drug (a drug that breaks up the blood clot blocking the brain), people treated within 3 hours did best: 33 in 100 recovered with little or no disability, against 23 in 100 without it. After 4.5 hours those trials showed no clear gain. The drug also caused a deadly bleed in the brain in about 3 in 100 people, against fewer than 1 in 100 without it, which is why hospitals scan the brain first. Other stroke treatments have other time limits. Sooner is better for all of them.

Infection

  1. Why this line is here

    This can be sepsis: the body being overwhelmed by an infection. It is on this list because it can come with no fever. In older people with a serious infection, fever is missing or weak 20 to 30 times in 100. So watch for confusion, fast breathing, and blotchy or cold skin, not the thermometer.

    Sepsis is involved in about 1 in 5 deaths worldwide (11 million deaths among 49 million cases in 2017). In 49,331 hospital patients in New York, each hour's delay in getting antibiotics (infection-fighting drugs) went with a slightly higher risk of dying.

Body

    • Use an adrenaline pen (an allergy auto-injector) now if you have one. Why: it is made for exactly this, and help may take time to arrive.
    • Cool down while you call. Don't wait for a thermometer. Why: the guides warn that above 105°F you can have seizures.

Pregnancy and the year after

  1. Why the year after the birth

    Some dangers of pregnancy come after the birth, up to a year later. Of US deaths caused by pregnancy in 2011 to 2015, about 1 in 3 came between one week and one year after the birth, and about 3 in 5 could have been prevented. The leading causes were heart conditions, infection and bleeding.

    • Call your maternity unit (the hospital team looking after your pregnancy) now. Why: they can check the baby the same day.
    • If you can't reach them, call the emergency number (911 in the US, 112 in Europe, 999 in the UK). Why: this should not wait until they answer.

Drugs and medicines

    • The poison line (US 1-800-222-1222) can tell you what to do. Keep the bottle or packet. Why: the label tells them exactly what was taken.
  2. Why this line is here

    Stopping heavy daily drinking or benzodiazepines suddenly can cause seizures. These withdrawals can kill. Most people who stop drinking do not get this: across 14 hospital studies, 1,355 of 71,295 patients at risk did. That is a count of hospital patients, not anyone's personal odds.

    The guides say it too: Benzos can be dangerous to quit cold turkey.

Mind and safety

    • Call the emergency number now (911 in the US, 112 in Europe, 999 in the UK), or get someone to call. Why: you deserve help right now, not later.
    • Get another person with you now, and put distance between you and the means. Why, in the guides' words: get another human being involved, and put some distance between you and the means.
    • Reach a person now: someone you trust, or a crisis line. US: call or text 988. Elsewhere: findahelpline.com. Why, in the guides' words: a crisis line can work even if the person on the other end is a total stranger.
    • If you can call safely, call the emergency number (911 in the US, 112 in Europe, 999 in the UK). Why: your safety comes before anything on this page.

It happened, and it passed

  • Call now, even though it passed

    Sudden weakness, a drooping face, trouble speaking, or sudden loss of sight or balance can warn of a stroke, even if it stops within minutes.

    The first days after carry the most risk; in one UK study, fast care cut strokes in the next 3 months from 10 in 100 to 2 in 100.

    Many callers turn out not to have had a stroke, often about half, and that is expected.

    A false alarm usually means a scan and some tests; a missed warning can mean a stroke.

    • Phone an emergency department or a stroke clinic now and say: possible warning stroke. Why: those words tell them what to check, and UK guidance asks for a stroke specialist within 24 hours.
    • Go where they send you. Why: they know which place can do the tests soonest.
    • Don't drive yourself. Why: the signs could come back at the wheel.
    • If any sign comes back, call the emergency number (911 in the US, 112 in Europe, 999 in the UK). Why: signs happening now are line 4, and some treatments then work only within hours.
    Why call when it has passed, in numbers

    A warning stroke means stroke signs that pass. After one, about 5 in 100 people have a stroke within a week. It was 11 in 100 where nobody treated it urgently, and 1 in 100 where a specialist stroke service treated it as an emergency (18 studies, 10,126 people).

    The UK study was in Oxford. Seeing people within a day instead of about three, and starting treatment at once, went with a fall in strokes over the next 3 months from 10 in 100 to 2 in 100. It compared before with after; it was not a fair test where chance decides who gets what.

    Why a false alarm is expected: the face, arm and speech signs catch strokes, but they do not rule out other causes. Of 100 people who call after signs pass, 40 to 67 turn out to have had a warning stroke or a small stroke (three studies). The others usually get a brain scan and tests, and often learn the real cause, such as an infection, a faint or a migraine. The clot-dissolving drug is given only while signs last, so people whose signs have passed almost never get it.

    Most strokes give no signs anyone notices: one US estimate counted about 11 million silent strokes (a 1998 estimate), against 770,000 with signs. This line is about the ones that do give signs.

    The guides say the larger risk is losing a treatment window or missing a bleed because the symptoms eased: losing the hours when a treatment still works, or missing bleeding in the brain.

    If it was more than a week ago, it is less pressing, but UK stroke guidance still asks for a specialist within seven days.

    What to ask when you are seen

    Why ask: in the guides' words, Calling for help is not the same thing as receiving good care.

    • What caused it? If you don't know yet, what test comes next?
    • What treatment starts now, while you look for the cause?
    • My heart rhythm: a heart tracing, and a monitor worn for days if the tracing is normal.
    • My neck arteries: an ultrasound (a sound-wave picture) or a CT scan (a detailed X-ray picture), looking for a narrowing.
    • A brain scan: was it a clot, a bleed, or neither?
    • My blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol.
    • What lowers my risk this week, and what are the risks of that?

Here about a child, or someone else? This page is for adults answering for themselves. A baby under 3 months with a fever of 38°C (100.4°F) needs to be seen now. Anyone hard to wake, blue, or struggling to breathe: call the emergency number.

Get seen today: signs that should not wait for a routine appointment

Any of these? Get seen today: a same-day doctor, urgent care, or an emergency department. Not a routine appointment. Why: these can turn serious within days.

If it gets worse while you wait, call the emergency number. Why: getting worse moves it onto the list above.

  • Black, tarry stools, or blood in the stool.
  • Coughing up streaks of blood.
  • A fever or chest infection that got better, then worse.
  • Tooth pain with a swollen face, or with fever: a dentist or a doctor, today.
    Why the tooth line

    A tooth infection left alone can spread. In US hospital records over nine years, 66 people died among 61,439 hospital stays for an infected tooth root. How many untreated infections end in hospital was not measured.

  • Yellow eyes or skin, with pale stools or dark urine.
  • Days without sleep; or, new for you, racing energy with no need for sleep, or hearing or seeing things others don't.
  • Nodding off at the wheel. Don't drive while you are this sleepy.

Before you stop daily drinking, benzodiazepines or any prescribed medicine, get a plan from whoever prescribes it or a clinic. Don't stop suddenly by yourself. Why: stopping some of these suddenly can cause seizures.

On every suggestion

  • Pick one. Try it for two weeks. Stop if you feel worse. Why: one change at a time shows you what helped.
  • Start everything at the lowest dose, and build up only as you are fine with it. Why, in the guides' author's words: it's allergy & sensitivity testing, common sense for everything.
  • Anything you might later breathe in as a mist, only smell first. Go on to the mist only once smelling it is fine. Why: smelling is the lowest dose; in his words, you don't start nebulizing (breathing a fine mist from a machine) until you are fine with smelling it.
  • Nothing on the get-help lists waits two weeks. Why: those signs need help now or today.

Where I live

From the detox guide, A few preliminaries.

What do you drink most days?
Any damp, mold or musty smell at home?
Is your sense of smell weaker than it was?

Filter the water you drink.

Quick fixToday, drink from a source you have checked: a good filter, or tested spring water.
Root causeFind out what is in your tap water. Then fit what removes it.
Why

In the guide's words, tap water is almost never drinkable these days. He names a good R/O system (reverse osmosis: a filter that pushes water through a very fine sheet), distilled water (but add minerals sometimes), or tested spring/well/rain water.

Label

Personal field signal.

Background

In 2015 nearly 21 million Americans were on water systems that broke health standards. At least one PFAS chemical ("forever chemicals": man-made chemicals that barely break down) can be found in about 45% of US tap samples. These show that contamination is common. They do not show "almost never drinkable", and they say nothing about your tap.

Where mainstream differs

Regulators call water that meets their standards safe.

Risk

Everyday.

Stop breathing the mold first. Start with the bedroom.

Quick fixTonight, open the windows if the air and the street outside allow. If not, run a true HEPA filter (a certified fine-dust air filter) by the bed.
Root causeFind the damp and dry it out. Get a humidity meter.
  • Open windows only if the street is safe and the outside air is clean. Why: his own condition for the open window is if you're not worried about criminals or outside pollution.
  • Don't scrub or tear out mold yourself without a plan. Why: it stirs the mold into the air; in his words, clearing it can backfire if not done intelligently.
Why

Prevent breathing in the mold that's there. The cheapo fix is just to open all the windows and doors. Mold grows best above 50-60% humidity. Below 40%, the moist lining of the nose dries out. Wash stored clothes and linens. Check the air conditioner, which can hold mold.

Label

Personal field signal.

Background

Homes with damp or mold go with 30-50% more breathing and asthma problems. That is an observed link; it does not test these steps.

Risk

Everyday.

Clear the smells that hide other smells.

Quick fixToday, no air fresheners or scented laundry products. Swap the dish sponge for a nylon brush.
Root causeFind what dulled it: mold, sinus trouble, or rancid cooking oil.
  • If you rinse your nose, use a sealed sterile product, or water boiled and then cooled; never water straight from the tap. Why: tap water in the nose has, rarely, carried a brain infection.
Why

Stop using dish sponges, switch to nylon brushes. For sinus trouble he rinses the nose: Nasal irrigation with Quinton Seawater (a seawater product).

Label

Personal field signal. The nose rinse: Limited human evidence (two small trials of salt-water rinsing in 116 adults; seawater itself was not tested).

Background

Among 3,005 Americans aged 57 to 85, those who could not smell died at four times the rate over the next five years. That is an observed link; it does not show that clearing scents helps.

Where mainstream differs

A loss of smell that lasts deserves a doctor's look.

Risk

Everyday. The nose rinse: Small. In one six-month trial of a strong salt-water rinse, 23 in 100 had side effects, nosebleeds among them. The brain infection by tiny water organisms (amoebas) after nose rinsing: 10 US cases from 1994 to 2022, all in people with weak immune systems, many using tap water. No rate per rinse exists.

Sleep

From the sleep guide. His order: First, thing's first, though, which is fixing underlying medical problems.

Do you snore, or has anyone heard you stop breathing or gasp in your sleep?
Sleepy by day?
Trouble falling asleep or staying asleep?
Do you take caffeine (coffee, tea, cola, energy drinks) or nicotine?

Nodding off at the wheel? Don't drive while you are this sleepy.

Why, in the guide's words: sleepy driving is as dangerous as drunk driving.

Check for sleep apnea (breathing that stops and starts in sleep) before anything else.

Quick fixAsk for a sleep test. Record your breathing tonight as a first clue. A prescribed breathing machine (CPAP: it blows air through a mask to keep the airway open) can ease sleepiness from the first night.
Root causeWork on why the airway closes: weight, alcohol and sedatives (calming or sleeping pills), mouth breathing, the shape of the airway.
  • Ask for a sleep test even if your recording sounds quiet. Why: a quiet recording does not rule apnea out.
Why

You can do your own sleep study with a simple microphone; then confirm the apnea subtype and severity. He calls the machine not a true cure, and lists what to consider first: Lose weight if overweight, less alcohol and sedatives, Yin yoga (slow yoga with long, held stretches), consider myofunctional therapy (exercises for the tongue, mouth and face), and fixing what blocks the airway.

Label

The machine: Limited human evidence. In before-and-after studies the crash rate was about 70% lower on it, and sleepiness eased after one night. That is not a promise for any one person. His list for the root cause: Personal field signal.

Background

Drivers with sleep apnea crash 1.2 to 4.9 times as often (studies that observed drivers, pooled).

Risk

Everyday.

Fix tonight's room. Then build the baseline.

Quick fixTonight: lights off, cool room, screens off a few hours before bed, write down what is on your mind.
Root causeKeep a two-week log. Then, in his order: same wake time every day, no caffeine, morning sun.
  • On a prescribed sleep medicine? Don't stop it suddenly; ask whoever prescribed it how to stop. Why: stopping some sleep medicines suddenly can be dangerous. The guide: Do not abruptly stop prescribed medication to improve sleep.
  • Stopping caffeine? Expect a headache for a few days. Why: it is common and passes; knowing that helps you not give up.
His fast one: a bag of zeolite in the pillow

Put a bag of it in your pillow! He means cleaned zeolite, a mineral powder, in a bag against the skin. In his words, nobody has studied this skin-contact-thru-bag method. Risk: no harm found, not studied. Dust from a leaking bag has not been measured.

Why

track the basics for 14 days. before taking anything for sleep, first build a baseline. Keep a consistent wake time. Avoid all stimulant drugs, including caffeine, completely, 100%. Let sunrise wake you.

Label

Personal field signal. Not checked yet: a fixed wake time is one part of the standard talking-and-habits treatment for poor sleep (called CBT-I); by itself it was not checked here.

Where mainstream differs

Morning caffeine is usually thought fine.

Risk

Everyday. Stopping caffeine: Small. In experiments, about half of regular users got a headache on stopping, and 13 in 100 had distress or trouble functioning that the researchers counted as significant. It starts within a day and lasts 2 to 9 days.

Not in this pilot: Vagal Blitz is the insta-fix for sleep, a fast, strong breathing method. It is not gentle, and comes later.

Stress and breathing

From the breathwork, somatic and inner child guides, and the guides' author's own notes.

How much stress on most days, from 0 (none) to 10 (the worst)?
Do you often breathe through your mouth, or sigh a lot?

Quick fix. Stressed right now? Breathe out slowly through pursed lips. Don't breathe in more to make up.

  • Don't hold your breath. Why: this practice only slows the breath out; it never stops it.
  • Ease off if you feel dizzy or tingly. Why: it means the breathing got too big, and it passes when the breathing settles.
  • Prone to panic? Go gentler. Why: the first seconds of slight air hunger can feel like panic.
How it feels, as he writes it

The slight air hunger feels a bit anxiogenic for the first 20 seconds or so (it makes you a little anxious); after that, you'll feel much calmer. People differ.

What it is

This is Buteyko reduced breathing. You can use this any time.

Risk

No harm found, not studied for this use.

Root cause. Change how you breathe all day. Step 1: Buteyko. Step 2: Systema.

Why this is the root-cause door

In the guide's words, over-breathing is one of the root causes of poor sleep and anxiety.

Step 1: Buteyko. Breathe a little less, through the nose, as a habit.

Buteyko is a method of breathing a little less than you feel you need, through the nose.

  • Prone to panic? Go gentler at first. Why: breathing less brings a slight air hunger, which can feel like panic.
Why it comes first

It is much easier for people to understand.

Label

Limited human evidence, for asthma only: breathing exercises improved quality of life (22 trials, 2,880 people; two of the trials used Buteyko), and a 60-person Buteyko trial cut inhaler use by about a fifth. For calming and sleep: Personal field signal; no trial was found.

Risk

No harm found, not studied for this use. The asthma trials recorded no harm, but their review did not count side effects.

Step 2: Systema. Breathe lightly and never pause. Keep it going under everything you do.

Systema is a martial art built on relaxed breathing. Its breathing has no pause between breathing in and breathing out.

  • Learn it sitting or lying down first. Why: beginners sometimes breathe too big and get dizzy.
  • Once it is light and steady, keep it going all day. Why: in his words, it's a background process.
How, in his words

light natural breathing. Each breath is 45-80% of a full deep breath: you don't fully exhale, you don't fully inhale either. the only control is that you make sure you are not pausing. Feel where the body is tense, and imagine the breath dissolving it.

Why

even better than just breathing less is circular breathing, Systema style. For anxiety: Systema continuous breathing is great! Most people try it just barely and forget it, so the buttons below keep a dated log of what you tried.

The video he points people to

The one on his somatic guide; he is not sure it is the best one: youtube.com/watch?v=Nar2-nvomAk. It opens on YouTube, online.

Label

Personal field signal (his, and the Systema community's). No studies found.

Risk

No harm found, not studied. If light breathing turns big and fast: dizziness and tingling, which pass when the breathing settles.

Later, with someone you trust: painless taps while you keep breathing. The fear is the practice.

  • The person receiving closes their eyes. Why: in his words, so they really can't cheat: they can't see whether the next tap is coming.
What

Light taps to the belly, or light slaps to the face that don't hurt. They are PAINLESS. You don't know whether the next one will land: even if the strikes don't land, it can still work usually.

Why

the idea is to do something that instills fear, with safety ensured. You learn to stay relaxed through fear. so it's like desensitization therapies (therapies that get you used to a fear step by step), without going back to a painful memory first.

Label

Personal field signal. He says i'm also not a systema master yet, and there is no written guide for this yet.

Risk

No harm found, not studied. Painless is the intent; a tap can land harder than meant, and fear can stir old fear. No rate exists for either.

The labels, in the guides' own terms
    
    
      
  • Established: standard treatment with strong evidence.
    • 
      
  • Limited human evidence: clinical reports or small trials but no strong modern outcome evidence.
    • 
      
  • Mechanism or preclinical evidence: effects shown mainly in cells or animals.
    • 
      
  • Personal field signal: what I have repeatedly observed in myself or other people. It does not turn into a rate.
    • 
      
  • Not checked yet: not looked up for this page.
    • 
      
  • Where mainstream differs: what most doctors or regulators say instead. It states a position and nothing more.
    • 
    

    
A label speaks only to the suggestion: does doing this help? A link that studies have observed goes under Background and labels nothing.

  

  
The risk words
    
    
      
  • Everyday: widely done or tested, with no serious harm on record.
    • 
      
  • No harm found, not studied: no report of harm was found at this use, and nobody has measured it. It does not mean safe.
    • 
      
  • Small: short-lived side effects are common, or a rare serious harm has a known cause that can be avoided.
    • 
    

    
Finding no report of harm is not proof of safety.
