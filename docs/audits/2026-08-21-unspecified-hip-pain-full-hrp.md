# Unspecified hip pain: full HRP evidence synthesis

## Status and scope

This is a completed, diagnosis-contingent evidence synthesis for the
de-identified prompt `how can i fix my bad hip`. It does not infer a diagnosis
from that wording and is not an individualized prescription. The practical
answer is diagnosis first: the same symptom label can describe joint
osteoarthritis, gluteal tendinopathy or bursitis, a labral or impingement
problem, referred lumbar or knee pain, inflammatory disease, osteonecrosis,
fracture, infection, or another process whose treatment differs materially.

The synthesis applies repository HRP `20.5.18` and Universal `20.5.14`.
`DIRECT_HUMAN`, `EXTENDED_GREY`, `FORUM_SIGNAL`, `BIDIRECTIONAL_ITERATION`, and
`FINAL_COMPLETION_AUDIT` were required and completed. The only terminal access
boundary is creator-content transcription: the installed YouTube Conversation
skill requires the user's Chrome transcript panel, and no Chrome-control tool
was available. Creator claims were therefore withheld. That boundary does not
block the synthesis because the answer rests on formal sources and audited
community episodes, not on unverified video content.

## Bottom line

1. **Rule out urgent or time-sensitive causes.** Sudden severe pain without an
   injury, a hot or swollen hip, skin colour change, fever or systemic illness
   needs urgent assessment. Severe pain after a fall, inability to walk or bear
   weight, or post-injury numbness or tingling needs emergency assessment. The
   NHS also advises clinical review when pain impairs normal activity or sleep,
   worsens or recurs, persists beyond two weeks of home care, or morning
   stiffness lasts more than 30 minutes.
2. **Identify the pain generator before choosing a remedy.** A targeted history
   and examination should distinguish groin or joint pain from lateral tendon
   pain and referred back or knee pain. For chronic hip pain, ACR considers hip
   and pelvis radiographs the usual first imaging after examination; MRI or
   ultrasound is selected later when radiographs are negative or a specific
   soft-tissue or intra-articular question remains.
3. **For a nonurgent, mild-to-moderate mechanical or osteoarthritis pattern, use
   a time-bounded, diagnosis-matched rehabilitation trial.** Education, load
   management, progressive strengthening or aerobic work, and an aid when
   needed are reasonable low-cost, reversible first steps. They are not a
   promise to rebuild cartilage. The 2026 Cochrane update found only small
   average hip-OA improvements that were unlikely to be clinically meaningful;
   a condition-specific trial found substantially better results for an exact
   education-and-loading programme in MRI-confirmed gluteal tendinopathy.
4. **Use medication or injections to support function, not to obscure a failed
   strategy indefinitely.** Oral NSAIDs have the strongest hip-OA medication
   support when safe, but gastrointestinal, renal, hepatic, cardiovascular,
   pregnancy, interaction, and comorbidity risks must be considered.
   Corticosteroid injections can provide short-term relief, but estimates vary,
   benefits are not reliably sustained beyond three months, post-injection
   flares occur, and rapidly progressive OA is a reported but causally uncertain
   safety signal. Hyaluronic-acid hip injections are not better than placebo in
   the AAOS evidence review.
5. **Do not treat supplements, diets, devices, PRP, or stem cells as proven hip
   regeneration.** A two-year hip-specific randomized trial found glucosamine
   no better than placebo for symptoms or joint-space loss. PRP evidence is
   inconsistent and formulation-dependent; ongoing comparative trials still
   have no results. Hip stem-cell evidence is small and heterogeneous. Exact
   PubMed probes found no hip-OA randomized trials for ketogenic/nightshade
   elimination, castor oil, Cissus, far-infrared heat, or traction, and the only
   photobiomodulation RCT found was an 18-person immediate post-arthroplasty
   incision study, not an at-home chronic hip-pain trial.
6. **When moderate-to-severe hip OA is confirmed, quality of life is substantially
   impaired, nonoperative care has failed, and a surgeon finds an indication,
   replacement has much stronger direct evidence than further generic exercise.**
   Two randomized trials show clinically important superiority at six and 12
   months. Surgery still has real early pain, dislocation, infection, nerve,
   leg-length, persistent-pain, and revision tradeoffs. Once a suitable patient
   has failed appropriate nonoperative care, ACR/AAHKS conditionally recommends
   proceeding rather than delaying solely for more PT, aids, NSAIDs, or
   injections.

## Diagnosis and safety gate

### Immediate escalation

- Emergency assessment: severe pain after trauma, inability to walk or bear
  weight, or numbness/tingling after injury.
- Urgent assessment: sudden severe atraumatic pain, a hot or swollen joint,
  colour change, fever, or feeling systemically unwell.
- Prompt non-emergency assessment: pain affecting sleep or ordinary activity,
  progressive or recurrent symptoms, failure to improve after two weeks of
  self-care, prolonged morning stiffness, unexplained weight loss or night pain,
  or meaningful loss of strength or function.

These thresholds are drawn principally from the current
[NHS hip-pain pathway](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/hip-pain/) and are triage
rules, not a diagnosis.

### Minimum assessment before a treatment verdict

Record onset and trauma, exact pain location, radiation, night and morning
pattern, fever or systemic symptoms, walking and weight-bearing ability, range
of motion, weakness, clicking or locking, lumbar and knee symptoms, steroid or
alcohol exposure relevant to osteonecrosis, inflammatory history, and medication
contraindications. A clinician can then decide whether plain radiographs are
appropriate and whether later MRI, ultrasound, laboratory testing, or referral
is justified. ACR's current pathway calls radiography the usual first imaging
test after clinical examination for chronic hip pain
([ACR criteria](https://acsearch.acr.org/docs/69425/Narrative/)).

### Exercise is not one intervention

The 2026 Cochrane update included 18 trials and 1,368 people with hip OA. Versus
no treatment/usual care/limited education, exercise improved pain by 7.19 points
and function by 8.79 points on 0–100 scales, changes the authors judged unlikely
to be clinically meaningful. Versus attention placebo, pain benefit was
uncertain and function improved slightly. This supports offering a tolerable,
individualized trial without promising that exercise reverses advanced disease
([PMID 42486497](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42486497/)).

The result cannot be transferred wholesale to lateral hip tendinopathy. In the
LEAP trial, 204 adults with more than three months of lateral pain, clinical and
MRI-confirmed gluteal tendinopathy received 14 physiotherapy-led education and
exercise sessions over eight weeks, one corticosteroid injection, or wait and
see. Global success at eight weeks was 51/66, 38/65, and 20/68 respectively;
education and exercise remained superior for global improvement at 52 weeks
([PMID 29720374](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29720374/)). The programme,
population, and diagnosis—not the generic label “glute exercise”—are essential.

### Injections have conflicting estimates

In the 199-person HIT trial, advice and education plus ultrasound-guided
triamcinolone/lidocaine improved average pain over six months by 1.43 points on
a 0–10 scale versus advice and education alone. It did not significantly beat
lidocaine injection, and ultrasound synovitis or effusion predicted a larger
response ([PMID 35387783](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35387783/)). NICE
therefore frames corticosteroid as a 2–10-week adjunct
([NG226](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng226/chapter/Recommendations)).

Meta-analyses disagree: a 2020 placebo-network analysis found steroid, PRP, and
hyaluronic acid no better than saline at two to four or six months
([PMID 32829298](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32829298/)), whereas a 2024
network analysis found steroid better than placebo for pain and function at
three months but not pain at six months
([PMID 38821500](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38821500/)). A 2023 safety
meta-analysis estimated rapidly progressive OA after hip steroid injection at
6% (95% CI 3%–9%) but emphasized variable definitions, missing follow-up, and
selection bias, so causality and the true incidence remain unknown
([PMID 37941925](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37941925/)). This supports
informed, limited use—not the categorical claim that every injection destroys a
hip, nor the claim that it is harmless.

### Severe-stage direct comparisons favour replacement

PROHIP randomized 109 adults aged at least 50 with severe OA and an indication
for surgery. At six months, Oxford Hip Score improved 15.9 points with
replacement and 4.5 with resistance training, a difference of 11.4 points
(95% CI 8.9–14.0); 21% assigned to training had crossed to replacement
([PMID 39476341](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39476341/)). A separate 2026
trial randomized 120 people with radiographic grade 2–4 OA: at 12 months the
between-group improvements favoured replacement by 27.1 WOMAC points and 24.1
Harris Hip Score points, with 38% of the exercise group undergoing replacement
during follow-up
([PMID 42061873](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42061873/)). These results do
not apply to undiagnosed pain or to every person with a mild radiographic change.

A 2026 synthesis of 1,904,237 modern replacements estimated 93.6% implant
survival without revision at 20 years and extrapolated 92.1% at 30 years; the
30-year value is a modelled estimate, not observed follow-up for every implant
([PMID 41763743](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41763743/)). ACR/AAHKS advises
that once moderate-to-severe OA or osteonecrosis is symptomatic, nonoperative
care has failed, and replacement is indicated, further delay solely for another
course of PT, aids, NSAIDs, or injections has no established benefit
([2023 timing guideline](https://rheumatology.org/press-releases/new-guideline-introduces-recommendations-for-optimal-timing-of-elective-hip-or-knee-arthroplasty-)).

### Regeneration and supplement claims

The strongest hip-specific supplement test randomized 222 people with hip OA to
1,500 mg glucosamine sulfate or placebo for two years. WOMAC pain, function, and
joint-space narrowing did not differ
([PMID 18283204](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18283204/)). A collagen review
found moderate-to-very-low-quality mixed-OA evidence and insufficient support
for generalized use
([PMID 22521757](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22521757/)). A newer
multi-joint collagen trial did not report a decisive hip-specific subgroup or
structural outcome, so it does not overturn the hip-specific conclusion.

For PRP, one five-RCT review reported improvements versus hyaluronic acid but
also small samples, variable preparation, and low-to-high risk of bias
([PMID 39569300](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39569300/)); the placebo-network
result above is less favourable. For stem-cell procedures, ten studies covering
316 patients reported promising symptoms and imaging but were too heterogeneous
and low quality for a definitive guideline
([PMID 39254726](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39254726/)). Active registry
records reinforce the uncertainty: PRP-versus-steroid, leukocyte-rich versus
leukocyte-poor PRP, and bone-marrow-concentrate-versus-triamcinolone studies had
no posted results at retrieval.

### Decision-critical full-text audit

The AAOS guideline and the open full texts of LEAP and HIT were inspected rather
than relying on abstracts. LEAP was publicly funded, reported no interested
financial relationships, and used MRI-confirmed gluteal tendinopathy while
excluding hip OA and low-back complaints. Its authors nevertheless identify
unblinded, mostly patient-reported outcomes, unequal clinician contact, and
exploratory secondary comparisons as limitations. The programme combined
education about avoiding compressive tendon positions with gradual loading and
daily hip-abductor/dynamic-control exercises, so its active component cannot be
reduced to one generic exercise.

HIT was publicly funded and disclosed one investigator's unrelated industry
trial funding. Its full text records that 94% of participants preferred an
injection, the primary advice-versus-injection comparison was unblinded and
self-reported, the injection-to-injection and moderator comparisons were
probably underpowered, and no saline placebo or radiographic outcome was used.
It also reports one death from subacute bacterial endocarditis four months after
injection that investigators deemed possibly treatment-related. Those details
support a cautious short-term-adjunct interpretation rather than an unqualified
endorsement.

PROHIP, the 2026 replacement trial, and the Cochrane update were not available
as complete open full text through the accessible sources. Their comprehensive
abstracts remain decision-relevant, but none is the sole basis for the ranking:
the replacement conclusion is triangulated by two independent randomized
comparisons plus guideline review, and the modest exercise conclusion is
triangulated by NICE, AAOS, and a second current review. This lowers the
materiality of the access gap without pretending it does not exist.

### Independent communities

Independent Reddit and Patient.info searches reproduced the directional spread:

- replacement discussions included both major quality-of-life improvement and
  regret, difficult recovery, persistent pain, and revision;
- hip steroid discussions ranged from no benefit or a short anaesthetic-only
  response, through flare and new pain, to relief lasting months;
- PRP discussions included delayed improvement, no effect, post-procedure pain,
  high out-of-pocket cost, and people moving directly to replacement; and
- diet/supplement discussions included perceived keto-related hip relief and a
  separate report of no glucosamine/chondroitin benefit.

Useful examples are the opposing trajectories in a
[replacement-regret discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/TotalHipReplacement/comments/1m9ey7x/what_have_you_lost_because_of_your_hip_replacement/),
the mixed experiences in a
[hip-injection discussion](https://patient.info/forums/discuss/45-and-osteoarthritis-of-the-hip-total-hip-replacment-help--547587),
and benefit/no-effect uncertainty in a
[PRP discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/Osteoarthritis/comments/1q4vcr3/i_got_prp_in_my_hip_heres_what_happened/).
These are firsthand discovery sources, not efficacy estimates.

### Bidirectional transfers

Community-to-formal transfer changed the analysis in five places:

1. Glute-frequency and walking stories triggered an exact gluteal-tendinopathy
   programme search; LEAP supported education and progressive loading only in a
   confirmed, condition-specific population.
2. Reports of exercise failure in dysplasia, avascular necrosis, and severe
   bone-on-bone disease triggered stage-bounded replacement comparisons;
   PROHIP and the 2026 trial showed why severe, surgery-indicated OA must not be
   averaged with mild disease.
3. Injection flare, rebound, and glucose reports triggered efficacy and
   rapidly-progressive-OA searches; formal evidence supported short benefit but
   left harm incidence and causality uncertain.
4. Supplement cointerventions triggered component-specific searches; the
   hip-specific glucosamine trial was negative, and collagen did not establish
   hip structural repair.
5. PRP cost and regeneration narratives triggered placebo-comparator and active
   registry checks; evidence remained inconsistent and three decisive
   comparative trials had no results.

Formal-to-community transfer then searched for the same discriminators:
confirmed versus generic tendon pain, mild versus severe OA, temporary versus
durable steroid relief, PRP benefit versus failure and flare, and replacement
benefit versus persistent pain or revision. No community direction erased the
formal population boundary or changed the evidence ranking.

## Practical measurement and decision plan

For a nonurgent presentation, establish a baseline before changing several
things at once:

- average and worst pain on a 0–10 scale;
- uninterrupted walking time or distance and whether an aid is needed;
- stairs, sit-to-stand, socks/shoes, getting into a car, and side-lying sleep;
- number of nights woken and days using rescue medication;
- the exact exercise, dose, frequency, and delayed response over the next
  24–48 hours; and
- one validated score if practical, such as HOOS or Oxford Hip Score.

Review weekly. For a diagnosis-matched conservative trial, predefine two or
three functional goals and the clinician-agreed change that would count as
worthwhile. Reduce load and reassess if symptoms escalate rather than settle;
stop a movement that causes sharp pain, new neurologic symptoms, or sustained
loss of function. If there is no useful trend after roughly six to 12 weeks of
an adhered-to, correctly targeted programme—or if basic activities and sleep
remain severely impaired—reassess the diagnosis and escalate rather than
recycling generic exercises. A temporary injection response should be recorded
separately from durable function and should not reset the decision clock by
itself.

## Evidence-frontier and stopping audit

Two consecutive broader passes tested the main overlooked claims. Exact PubMed
queries returned zero hip-OA randomized trials for castor oil, ketogenic or
nightshade elimination, Cissus, far-infrared heat, or traction. The only red-
light record was the stage-mismatched 18-person immediate post-arthroplasty
study. Collagen results were mixed-site rather than decisive hip evidence, and
the apparent stem-cell “regeneration” search hits were predominantly knee
studies; the hip review remained heterogeneous and low-certainty. Neither pass
added a decision-changing option, discriminator, contradiction, or actionability
change.

Final completion audit:

- safety and diagnosis alternatives: complete;
- formal guidelines, systematic reviews, decisive trials, and registries:
  complete with explicit conflicting injection estimates;
- direct-human benefit, no-effect, harm, tolerability, adherence,
  discontinuation, and recovery directions: complete and non-quantitative;
- community-to-formal and formal-to-community iteration: complete;
- option, nonaction, cost, reversibility, time horizon, measurement, stopping,
  and escalation fields: complete;
- creator transcript verification: terminally access-bounded and excluded from
  evidentiary claims; and
- further expansion likely to change this diagnosis-contingent answer: **no**,
  absent a real patient's history, examination, imaging, preferences, or new
  trial results.

The operational recommendation is therefore not a universal hip routine. It is
an urgent-symptom screen, then a targeted assessment, a measured and
diagnosis-matched conservative trial when appropriate, and timely surgical
discussion when confirmed severe disease and disability make more delay a poor
trade.
