# The study map (UDA item 2026-10-31-study-map-for-decision-claims; owner request, 31 Oct 2026)

Status: designed on 5 Nov 2026; the owner said yes (answer 249a, 5 Nov). First version built on 5 Nov on
`claude/next-build-20261105`: `studies`, `links`, `claims` and checks 1 to 6, all notes, with `fetch_clinical_trial`'s
runtime `registration`, and reworked the same day on an independent review's ten findings (see "Built"). Left: the HRP rule (20.6.40, with its edit record and a protocol test pinning the core), a
regression run measuring how often the notes fire, the outcome comparison (`outcomes`), the meaning-judge job on
declared overlap, the panel line, and the owner's option of a seventh blocking check.

## What it is for

Errors that sit between studies, which a one-paper audit can't see: two reports of one group of people counted as two
(Tramèr 1997: covert duplicates inflated the ondansetron effect by 23%), a review restating a trial counted as a second
source, and a paper whose outcomes differ from its registration (COMPare 2019: 58 of 67 trials needed a correction).
HRP already states the rule in prose (`DuplicateAndDataLineageAudit`, `IdenticalResultsAcrossPublications`); the map
makes the exact parts checkable.

## Shape: `study_map`, a later input of `finalize_research`

The frozen tool lists don't change (owner answer 189a): it goes in `LATER_INPUT_SHAPES.finalize_research`
(`apps/research-mcp/src/later-inputs.ts`), with its schema and limits in a new `apps/research-mcp/src/study-map.ts`.

- `studies` (2 to 8): `id`, `source` (DOI, PMID, PMCID or URL), `group` (a label for the people behind it), and when
  known `registry` (up to 3 numbers), `sites`, `recruited`, `run_by`, `people`.
- `links` (up to 12): `from`, `kind` (`same_cohort`, `reanalysis_of`, `extension_of`, `abstract_of`,
  `cites_as_evidence`), `to`, `passage` (the sentence that shows the link).
- `claims` (1 to 4): `quote` (from the answer draft), `studies`, `groups` (how many independent groups of people the
  claim counts), `passages` (per study, the sentence the claim rests on).

The AI sends no handles or hashes: each `source` resolves through the research receipts exactly as `source_lines`
does, and a quote is looked up only in its own study's text, so a review can't anchor a trial's claim. Zod checks the
structure; cross-references are checked in code, so one bad item is dropped, never the whole map.

## Checks: all notes, after a passing check, consuming no rounds (owner answers 193a, 195a, 217b)

Exact comparisons only (the pattern-matching fit rule); whether two groups overlap, or whether a passage supports a
claim, stays the AI's judgment.

1. **A study tied to a receipt**, or its passages aren't looked up.
2. **Each passage found word for word** in its study's text (the source line's comparison, which works in any
   language), through a shared `lookUpQuotes()` taken out of `apps/research-mcp/src/source-lines.ts`.
3. **Each claim anchored:** its quote is in the draft, and every study it rests on gives a passage; an absence claim
   resting on two or more studies is in `claims`.
4. **A registry number tied** to its study: it appears in the study's text, or the registry record lists the study's
   PMID.
5. **Groups of people counted once:** studies with a declared link, the same `group`, the same registry number
   (declared, or found in an abstract), one registry record listing both, or identical unusual figures (the gate's
   existing test) form one group. Only over-counting is flagged: a claim that counts more groups than that.
6. **Registered late:** a ClinicalTrials.gov record first registered after the trial started gets a note to say so
   beside the result. `fetch_clinical_trial` keeps the record's outcomes, dates, sites and secondary IDs as a
   runtime-only output extension (as `get_youtube_comments` does), and the check fetches the record again itself.

Later, after v1 is measured: the outcome comparison (`outcomes`: registered against reported, left out, added or moved
between primary and secondary, unless the paper says why; against the current record, since the documented API serves
no version history), a meaning-judge job reading declared overlap (dev accounts only, measured first), and a panel
line "N papers from M groups of people".

**An option for the owner, not the plan:** make one exact case a seventh blocking check, a claim counting as
independent two studies whose abstracts name the same registry number. Recommendation: keep it a note in v1 and
measure how often it fires on the regression list first.

## The protocol rule (HRP 20.6.40, the wording put to the owner in question 249)

`StudyMapForCombinedEvidence`, in `ExtendedHumanEvidenceAndGreyLiteratureSweep`, right after
`DuplicateAndDataLineageAudit`, outside the core:

> When a conclusion combines studies, or rests on something not found or not shown, keep the
> DuplicateAndDataLineageAudit as a small study map in finalize_research's later_inputs.study_map, from what the
> research read. Give each study its identifier, its trial registry number if any, and the group of people behind it:
> where and when they were recruited, how many, and who ran or paid for it. Link a study that shares people with,
> reanalyses, extends or is an abstract of another, and a review to the study it cites as evidence, each with the
> sentence that shows the link. Count groups of people, not papers. Compare a registered trial's paper with its
> registration from before the trial started: outcomes left out, added, or moved between primary and secondary
> without a reason the paper gives. Say in the answer what the map finds. Whether two groups overlap stays your
> judgment.

## Tests (the item's nine cases)

`tests/study-map.test.ts` with fake texts and a fake record (NCT01234567: first submitted after its start date in one
variant). Two reports of one cohort counted as two (note); a review repeating a trial counted once (none) and twice
(note); a genuinely independent replication (none); a claim with a study but no passage (note); an absence claim left
out of `claims` (note); a registry number not found in its study (note); registered after the start (note); passages
in Japanese and French; a failed registry fetch (one note, the check stands); and the negative control: three
distinct trials, everything found, no note, and a check sent without a map returns exactly what it returns today.
The outcome cases (silently replaced, left out, secondary as primary, changed after the start) wait for the outcome
comparison. Unchanged and passing: `tests/source-lines.test.ts` (guards the refactor), the frozen tool lists, and the
owner's 195 tests. The HRP edit gets its edit record and a protocol test pinning the core.

## Built (first version, 5 Nov 2026)

Choices the design left open, as built, with the coordinator's decisions on two reviews of 5 Nov. Inferred joins kept
producing new wrong cases (a review or a dual registration merging trials, a master protocol's number joining two
trials, a gene name read as a registry number), each local rule fixing one case and opening another, because whether two
studies share people is a judgment of meaning. So the mechanism changed:

- **Groups come only from the AI's declarations:** a link of shared people (`same_cohort`, `abstract_of`,
  `extension_of`, `reanalysis_of`) to one study, or an equal `group` label. A study with a `cites_as_evidence` link
  out, or reanalysing or extending two or more studies, holds several groups: it joins none, and a claim resting on one
  gets no count. The over-count note fires only when the AI's declarations contradict its own count.
- **What AskRigor finds is a hint, never a join,** each a note worded as a question that names the link each answer
  calls for (`same_cohort` for the same people, `cites_as_evidence` for a review or a pooled analysis), since a review
  linked `same_cohort` to two trials would join them: two studies of one claim giving the same registration while the
  map neither groups nor links them; a ClinicalTrials.gov registration listing another map study among its results;
  identical audited figures (HRP IdenticalResultsAcrossPublications); and a registry number a study's abstract names
  that the map doesn't give for it. Such a number asks nothing when a study it links to gives it (a review naming the
  trials it cites), asks how the two relate when an unlinked study gives it, and otherwise goes, with every other such
  number, into one note for the map.
- **Registry numbers** are read only in strict, fixed-digit forms, each with its prefix (NCT, ISRCTN, EudraCT or CTIS
  with its label, ACTRN, ChiCTR, DRKS, CTRI, jRCT, UMIN, PACTR, KCT, TCTR, RBR); a Netherlands Trial Register number
  (NTR, NL, four or more digits) is accepted as declared and never looked for in a text; any other value is left out
  with a note. A declared number ties its study when its full text names it or its record lists the study's paper
  (RESULT and DERIVED references); an abstract can't show a number missing. The registered-late note is for a declared
  number its study's text and record don't contradict.
- **Passages:** a link's is looked up in the texts of the two studies it links, never a third; one too short to mean
  much gets the source line's note.
- **Time:** one 8-second deadline, the source lines'; the map's PubMed reads start after the source lines' and share
  their cache, and its ClinicalTrials.gov reads (declared NCT numbers, at most 8) start at once; what was read in time is
  kept, and what wasn't gives one note.
- **Places:** each item is read on its own, so notes and the runtime result name it by its place as given.
- **Shape:** described in result text only with a passing check of an answer on two or more key sources and no map.

## Risks

- **False notes:** exact copying fails on PDF text and registry wording; month-only dates compare only when
  unambiguous.
- **Burden:** the AI already sends large final-check inputs, and the run on #412's build had over a hundred schema
  refusals of study audits. The map is optional and small, and its shape is described in result text only when an
  answer has at least two key sources.
- **Under-declaring:** the AI can leave a link out; registry numbers found in abstracts counter that in part.
