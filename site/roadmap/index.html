Roadmap

What we're building.

AskRigor does the research behind an AI's answer, and keeps what people report and what studies show in one place. Here's what's being built now, next and later.

Now

  • Side-by-side comparisons: the same AI with and without AskRigor, on real questions. See the first one.

Next

  • A forum where nothing gets lost. People talk about what worked for them and research they've done. AI organizes every post: it counts each person once, checks the studies, and shows what's already known.
  • Your report joins similar ones. Before you post, AskRigor shows reports on the same remedy for the same problem and asks whether yours matches. If it does, yours adds to it instead of starting over.
  • Tests at the level of rigor you want: a simple two-week diary; on, off and on again with a measurement; or a blinded test with someone else preparing the doses. Each has safety rules, and your results come back as reports.
  • The most promising tests first. Each health category puts first the tests the community could run: well reported, plausible, safe and cheap.
  • "Did it last?" If you agree, reminders at 3, 6 and 12 months turn single reports into something closer to a study.
  • Good forums, by topic. AskRigor searches a list of trusted forums whenever the topic fits, such as LongeCity for supplements or Phoenix Rising for ME/CFS, and you'll be able to suggest the ones you trust. Each is checked before it's searched.
  • AskRigor in ChatGPT's app directory, so anyone, free plan included, can add it in a click.
  • Science news, checked. Daily headlines from ScienceAlert, EurekAlert! and other public feeds, each with what the study behind it shows: in people or animals, how many, compared with what, and who paid. Pick your categories, and read it on the site or by email.

Later

  • Figures read as images, so "full text and figures analyzed" can stand beside a study.
  • A loop that never stops learning: what people report leads to new study searches, and what studies find leads back to the people who tried it.
  • More comparisons, across questions and AI apps, Claude included.
  • Better thinking on any question, not only health: sources found and read, and the answer checked before you see it, whatever you ask.

Growing with you

AskRigor reads from services that limit how much anyone can ask of them: YouTube allows a set number of searches a day, PubMed a set number of requests a second, Reddit a set reading pace, and full-text papers come one request at a time. As more people use AskRigor, these limits are where it would slow down first. The plan:

  • Read once, reuse often. What AskRigor has already read and checked is kept and reused, so a question asked twice costs once.
  • Spread the work. Research that can wait runs over time instead of all at once.
  • Ask for more. As use grows, AskRigor asks each service for higher limits, as it already did with YouTube.
  • More sources. More forums, journals and libraries, so no single service is a bottleneck.

Why it works this way

The main choices behind AskRigor, and why. Open an area to read it.

Research and sources
  • A quick first pass, then an offer to go deeper. A full-depth answer used to take hours, so depth is something you choose.
  • The quick answer first. Before researching, the AI writes the answer it would give without AskRigor. The final answer ends by saying what the research confirmed or changed, so you can see what it added.
  • The short answer, and the safest options, come first.
  • Numbers you can picture. Results as people out of 100, a one-word grade for the evidence, a bottom line even when evidence is weak, and doubts said plainly ("I'm not sure"). Studies show people understand these best.
  • What each claim rests on. Beside each study, how it was read. Beside a key claim, the paper's own words, which AskRigor checks are really there.
  • One study isn't enough for a big claim. AskRigor looks for agreement across trials, people's reports and other evidence.
  • Honest about access. If a paper can't be fetched, the answer says which service failed. A study is never called "unavailable" because one route failed.
  • Not only health. AskRigor researches any field. Health is its focus.
The final check
  • Only six things can stop an answer: real sources, studies described as they were read, numbers that add up, safety claims backed by a search for harms, nothing taken back slipping back in, and an honest comparison with the quick answer.
  • Everything else becomes a note or a stated limit. Code can check exact things, not whether research is good, and a check that refused almost every answer helped no one.
  • A visible check. AskRigor can't see the final answer your app shows you, so it shows its check: a check line, and a folded panel with what was checked, every limit and the numbers it checked.
  • No endless loops. A check that can't pass stops after 10 rounds and says why.
  • Meaning is judged by AI, not word lists. Word lists miss paraphrase and negation, and fail in other languages. A second AI that reads meaning is being tested; it can only add notes.
The Atlas and its cards
  • Two layers. Research AskRigor keeps and reuses, so nothing is done twice, and public pages that show only what's valuable and hard to find.
  • Tested against a quick AI answer. A finding from AI research is shown only after a separate AI checks that a quick answer would miss it, and that it could change someone's mind about something important. An AI can't fairly judge its own work.
  • The whole picture. A shown card must carry everything needed to decide, with the new parts highlighted, so nothing is shown on that test alone until cards can hold it.
  • Labelled. A finding no person has reviewed says so.
  • Findings stay while they're valid. They're corrected with a dated note, or taken down when someone shows they're wrong. You can remove everything that ties you to a card, any time, with no reason needed.
  • Free and paid. Free research shares its checked findings. Paid private research saves nothing unless you say yes. Research only about you is never saved.
People's reports and forums
  • People's own reports first. On the Atlas, what worked for people comes first, like Earth Clinic, with AskRigor's research around each. A report that's safe to try publishes once AI checks pass, with a labelled AI warning where one is needed.
  • AskRigor reads forum threads itself, whole, with their replies. Usernames never reach the AI, so each person counts once and nobody is named.
  • People, not posts. Only people who used the exact thing count for it.
  • Reports can stand on their own. A study counts against them only if it measured the same thing.
  • A loop. What people report should lead to study searches, and what studies find should lead back to people's reports.
Video and YouTube
  • Nothing from YouTube comments on public pages until YouTube agrees. AskRigor told YouTube such counts stay inside people's own answers.
  • Within YouTube's rules. Searches use YouTube's official service and its daily limit. When the limit runs out, the answer says so and carries on.
  • Videos are read by Google's Gemini, which watches the public video. If a video couldn't be read, the answer says so.
Privacy and safety
  • Nothing kept by default. No request log, no copy of your quick answer, and no reading history on the Atlas.
  • Shared only with your yes. Your question and answer go to AskRigor to improve it only if you say yes and confirm the exact de-identified copy. Copies are deleted after 90 days.
  • No getting past bot checks, CAPTCHAs, logins or paywalls.
  • Serious harms are searched by name. A harm not searched is reported as not checked, never as absent.
  • Bug reports are shown to you first and sent only if you say yes.
  • Signing up never reveals whether an email already has an account.
Spending and AI models
  • In the AI app you already use, on your own plan. There's no separate web app for now: it would have to pay for every answer.
  • No paid AI calls of its own unless the owner approves a specific use with a ceiling.
  • When paid features exist, free users will be told what they'd add, and only where it's true for their app.
Releases and apps
  • A fixed tool list. Your app needs a refresh only when a tool is added or removed, and AskRigor tells you when.
  • Built for the ChatGPT app, the Claude connector and the Codex plugin, not Custom GPTs.
  • Not in ChatGPT's app directory yet. It waits until AskRigor works well enough.
  • Tested every release on a list of problems people have reported. Test runs never save findings.
This site and support
  • Better health information shouldn't depend on what you pay.
  • Support, not donations. Money you give supports AskRigor's development. It goes to Mayan Roots LLC, which builds AskRigor, and isn't tax-deductible.
  • Bigger research jobs are quoted by hand. Ask from the Support page; nothing is charged unless you accept the quote.
  • No scripts. These pages run none.
