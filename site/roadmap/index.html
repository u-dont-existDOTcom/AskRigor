Roadmap

What we're building.

AskRigor does the research behind an AI's answer, and keeps what people report and what studies show in one place. Here's what's being built now, next and later.

Now

  • The Atlas, open to everyone. Share what worked for you, Earth Clinic style. AskRigor researches each report and publishes it once its checks pass, with a caution where one is needed. Others can say "it worked for me too" or "it didn't", counted once per person, under a clear tree of health problems.
  • The research behind every finding. Each finding shows the papers' own sentences, how each paper was read, what people report about it, and what the studies did or didn't find.
  • What was really read, beside every study. An answer says, beside each study it cites, whether AskRigor read the abstract only, the full text, or the full text with its methods checked.
  • Side-by-side comparisons: the same AI with and without AskRigor, on real questions. See the first one.

Next

  • A forum where nothing gets lost. People talk about what worked for them and research they've done. AI organizes every post: it counts each person once, checks the studies, and shows what's already known.
  • Your report joins similar ones. Before you post, AskRigor shows reports on the same remedy for the same problem and asks whether yours matches. If it does, yours adds to it instead of starting over.
  • Tests at the level of rigor you want: a simple two-week diary; on, off and on again with a measurement; or a blinded test with someone else preparing the doses. Each has safety rules, and your results come back as reports.
  • The most promising tests first. Each health category puts first the tests the community could run: well reported, plausible, safe and cheap.
  • "Did it last?" If you agree, reminders at 3, 6 and 12 months turn single reports into something closer to a study.
  • AskRigor in ChatGPT's app directory, so anyone, free plan included, can add it in a click.

Later

  • Figures read as images, so "full text and figures analyzed" can stand beside a study.
  • A loop that never stops learning: what people report leads to new study searches, and what studies find leads back to the people who tried it.
  • Health news with better headlines: what was tested, the effect in plain numbers, what the study can't show, and who paid.
  • More comparisons, across questions and AI apps, Claude included.

Tell us what you think

Comments on this roadmap open with the Atlas, and AskRigor's AI reads each one into the plan. A suggestion that's clearly good goes in, and anything else is reviewed by a person first. Until then, write to joel@askrigor.com.
